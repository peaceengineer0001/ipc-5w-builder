# IPC Theory — How Inductive Pulse Charging Works

## Introduction

Inductive Pulse Charging (IPC) is a process that uses high-voltage electromagnetic transients — generated by the rapid switching of current through solenoid coils — to charge batteries in a way that demonstrates a Coefficient of Performance (CoP) greater than 1.

This document explains the core principles behind IPC so you understand what you are building and why it works.

---

## The Flyback Pulse

### Faraday's Law of Induction

When current flows through a coil, it creates a magnetic field. When that current is suddenly interrupted (switched off), the magnetic field collapses rapidly. Faraday's Law tells us that this rapidly changing magnetic field induces a voltage in the coil — and the faster the change, the higher the voltage.

This induced voltage is called a **flyback pulse** or **back-EMF**, and it can reach voltages far higher than the original supply voltage.

### Lenz's Law

Lenz's Law states that the direction of the induced EMF always opposes the change that produced it. This is nature's way of conserving energy — if the induced voltage supported the change, you would get runaway energy gain, violating conservation of energy as conventionally understood.

However, by engineering the timing and configuration of when currents flow and when they are interrupted, it is possible to work **with** Lenz's Law rather than against it, capturing the flyback energy productively.

### The Pulse in Detail

In the PFGen system:

1. A **PWM module** sends a square wave signal to the gate of a MOSFET
2. On the **rising edge**: the MOSFET turns on, current flows through the coils, magnetic fields build up
3. While conducting: the coils generate a Lenz-response pulse that is earthed through the MOSFET's parasitic diode
4. On the **falling edge**: the MOSFET turns off, current stops, magnetic fields collapse rapidly
5. This collapse generates a **high-voltage flyback pulse** (0.5 - 1.7kV depending on the active device)
6. The flyback pulse is directed through a diode to the **receiving battery**

### Key Pulse Parameters

| Parameter | Typical Value | Significance |
|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| Peak Voltage | 0.5 - 1.7 kV | Higher = stronger energy interaction |
| Pulse Width | 40 - 50 µs | Duration of the HV transient |
| Rate of Change (dV/dt) | ~1 × 10⁸ V/s | Critical factor in observed energy gain |
| PRF Range | 50 - 200 Hz | Battery-specific optimal frequency |

## Coefficient of Performance (CoP)

The CoP is the ratio of energy output to energy input:

**CoP = Energy Out / Energy In**

- CoP < 1: More energy goes in than comes out (normal for most systems)
- CoP = 1: Energy in equals energy out (100% efficient)
- CoP > 1: More energy comes out than goes in

The IPC system has demonstrated CoP values up to **4.22 ± 0.54** with a 7Ah LiFePO4 battery. This means for every unit of energy supplied, over 4 units of energy were measured in the battery.

## The Battery Swapper

The system uses two matched batteries that alternate roles:

- **Battery A** supplies power to the circuit and coils
- **Battery B** receives the flyback pulses and charges
- After a set interval (controlled by the CD4060 timer chip), the roles swap
- Battery B now supplies, Battery A receives pulses

In **closed-loop operation**, this swapping continues indefinitely. If the CoP is sufficiently high, the system sustains itself while also powering an external load (5W LED in the basic system).

## Magneto Motive Force (MMF)

The **Ampere-Turns (AT)** value of the coils determines the strength of the magnetic field, which directly impacts the energy influx:

**MMF = N × I** (Number of turns × Current in amps)

The key insight: to maximize CoP, you want to **maintain strong magnetic fields while minimizing supply current**. This is achieved by using more turns of wire rather than higher current — the magnetic field stays strong, but input power is reduced.

## Why LiFePO4 Batteries Respond Better

LiFePO4 batteries consistently show higher CoP values than lead-acid batteries:

1. **Lower internal resistance** — allows more efficient pulse absorption
2. **Higher Gibbs energy** of Li+ ions in the intercalated electrode structure
3. **Smaller atomic mass** of lithium vs. lead
4. **Many small discrete cells** vs. fewer large plates — may support pulse propagation effects

## The Energy Source Question

The origin of the additional energy measured in IPC systems is an open scientific question with two hypotheses:

1. **Enthalpy changes** within the battery electrochemistry — the HV pulses cause internal chemical changes that release stored energy
2. **Environmental energy** — the far-from-equilibrium conditions created by IPC may draw energy from the local environment through thermodynamically open-system processes

The second OSF study is designed to distinguish between these hypotheses using the Chemical Deficit Model.

## Summary

IPC works by:
1. Generating high-voltage flyback pulses from solenoid coils
2. Directing these pulses into batteries at specific frequencies and timing
3. Optimizing the operating parameters for maximum CoP
4. Using a battery swapper to enable continuous self-sustaining operation

The phenomenon is real, measurable, and replicable. This repository gives you everything needed to build and verify it yourself.
